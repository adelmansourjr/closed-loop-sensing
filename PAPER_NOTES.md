# Research notes for Petra Fischer's closed-loop sensing theme

## Bristol project and recent work

- The Bristol HLSUOB26 project **Advancing brain activity-based non-invasive vibrotactile stimulation for dystonia** names Petra Fischer, Alexander Harris and Ehsan Jafari. The complementary GW4 project led by Fischer also describes personalised continuous monitoring. The shared computational question is whether the recordings identify the state that matters for a decision. This project deliberately isolates state estimation with passive synthetic observations. [Bristol HLS advert](https://www.findaphd.com/phds/project/funded-phd-advancing-brain-activity-based-non-invasive-vibrotactile-stimulation-for-dystonia-hlsuob26/?p200923) · [GW4 project](https://gw4biomed.ac.uk/developing-brain-activity-based-non-invasive-stimulation-techniques-for-movement-disorders/).
- In June 2026, Fischer, Amy Rose Taylor and Conor Houghton reported progress on an AI-assisted personalisation collaboration. The institutional update connects wearable sensing, computational modelling and clinician-blinded assessment. It is a project progress report. The most direct connection to this pilot is the need to decide which information is useful enough to measure. [Elizabeth Blackwell Institute update](https://elizabethblackwellinstitute.blogs.bristol.ac.uk/2026/06/04/ai-optimised-personalised-neurostimulation-for-dystonia/).
- Winkler, Fischer and colleagues' 2025 eLife study compares starting, stopping and reversing a continuous movement under predictable and unpredictable cues. The local and cross-region synchronisation profiles differ across these contexts, and a momentary pause during a reversal does not produce the same clear rebound as stopping a sequence. Some responses vary across people. This made the assessment families for cue reliability and changes in phase dynamics more important than a single nominal accuracy number. The synthetic laws are illustrative and have no fitted correspondence to those observations. [eLife version of record](https://elifesciences.org/articles/101769).
- Anderton, Ashida, Fischer and Boca presented an observational dystonia study at a 2025 meeting. Its analysis of frequency-band power motivates keeping signal quality and biological endpoints distinct in discussion. It is a conference abstract. [Primary abstract](https://neurotarget.com/index.php/nt/article/view/571).

## Methodological anchors

- Wodeyar and colleagues' state-space estimator represents the varying signal as a latent state and reports phase uncertainty. Their result motivates modelling an observation process and scoring probability quality separately from the most likely phase sector. The pilot uses a finite hidden Markov model to permit exact policy enumeration. [State-space phase estimation](https://elifesciences.org/articles/68803).
- Zrenner and colleagues distinguish causally available phase estimates from a retrospectively processed reference. They show why reference uncertainty itself matters at weak amplitude or poor signal-to-noise ratio. This benchmark therefore uses latent simulation truth, explicit attenuation and erasure, and no future input to the online decoder. Extending the measurement experiment to physical signals would require an independent reference and a prespecified definition of which intervals are identifiable. [The shaky ground truth of real-time phase estimation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7284312/).
- Wodeyar and colleagues' later work finds stronger agreement among phase methods at low uncertainty. That points to a useful follow-up distinction between confidence in the class decision and confidence in the underlying continuous-angle estimate. In the present experiment, identical classifications accompany a worse Brier score with fewer readings. [Phase-method agreement study](https://www.eneuro.org/content/10/11/ENEURO.0507-22.2023).

## Related Cambridge advert

Cambridge's **Uncertainty-aware decision-making for adaptive neural stimulation** with Flavia Mancini asks which uncertainties matter for a decision in a partially observed system. Its methodological connection here is decision-relevant information. Our comparator asks whether a reading can change a classifier decision. It uses a passive synthetic signal and a known mapping from endpoint phase to labels. Mancini's lab lists a 2026 preprint on moment-to-moment variability and pain chronification. Her 2022 coauthored work more directly anchors the use of a probabilistic forecast and an explicit distinction between a prediction and its subsequent update. [Cambridge project advert](https://www.findaphd.com/phds/project/uncertainty-aware-decision-making-for-adaptive-neural-stimulation/?p200555) · [NOX Lab publications](https://www.noxlab.org/pubs/) · [Computational and neural mechanisms of statistical pain learning](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34283-9).

## What to discuss

Show the reviewer the exact two-read comparison first, then the stopping certificate. Ask which measurement decision in their work has a stable, independently verifiable reference, and whether the useful outcome is a hard class, calibrated probability or angle estimate. A measurement-only follow-up can precommit to an uncertainty tolerance on that outcome and compare it with the hard-label certificate under hidden rhythm changes and independently labelled signal quality.
